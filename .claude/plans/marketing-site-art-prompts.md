# Image prompts for the marketing site

Copy-paste prompts for ChatGPT (GPT-4o / DALL·E) and Meta AI, matching the
supplied reference style: faceted low-poly clay, warm cream ground, terracotta
and sage palette, soft studio light.

Referenced by [marketing-site.plan.md](marketing-site.plan.md) — the shot list
there says where each piece goes.

---

## The style block

**Every prompt below already includes this.** It is repeated here so you can
paste it onto anything extra you generate and stay on-style.

> Low-poly 3D render with visible flat polygon facets. Matte clay and painted
> wood materials, no gloss, no metal, no reflections. Warm cream background
> (#F5EFE3). Palette of terracotta orange, sage green, warm tan and soft
> off-white. Soft diffused studio lighting from the upper left, gentle contact
> shadows beneath objects, shallow depth of field with a softly blurred
> background. Handcrafted wooden toy aesthetic, rounded friendly forms, no
> text, no lettering, no words, no logos, no watermarks.

### How to use these

- **ChatGPT**: paste the whole prompt. It rewrites internally, so the prose
  phrasing matters more than keyword stuffing. If it drifts glossy or realistic,
  reply "make it more faceted and matte, like a carved wooden toy."
- **Meta AI**: use the **Short** variant where given — it responds better to
  dense, shorter prompts and tends to ignore long negative lists.
- **Ratios**: ChatGPT reliably gives 1:1, 3:2, 2:3. Ask in words ("wide
  landscape composition") and crop to the target ratio afterwards. Meta AI is
  mostly square — generate square and crop.
- **Text is the main failure.** These models love adding labels. Every prompt
  below ends with a no-text instruction; if lettering still appears, regenerate
  rather than trying to fix it.
- **Generate 3-4 of each** and keep the one whose facets and palette best match
  the Buddy dog reference — that piece is the style anchor for the whole set.

## 1. Hero — two dogs meeting

**Target: 4:3 landscape · the single most important image on the site**

> A low-poly 3D render of two friendly dogs meeting nose to nose on a small
> circular grass platform. One dog is a large golden retriever in warm tan, the
> other is a smaller terrier in cream and soft brown, so their size difference
> is clear. Both are built from visible flat polygon facets with a matte clay
> and painted wood finish — no gloss, no metal, no reflections. They stand on a
> faceted low-poly grass disc with sage green tones and a few simple geometric
> tufts and small rounded stones. Warm cream background (#F5EFE3), softly
> blurred. Soft diffused studio lighting from the upper left, gentle contact
> shadows under both dogs. Shallow depth of field. Handcrafted wooden toy
> aesthetic, rounded friendly forms, cheerful and warm. Wide landscape
> composition with generous empty cream space above the dogs. No text, no
> lettering, no words, no logos, no watermarks.

**Short (Meta AI):**
> Low-poly faceted 3D render, two dogs meeting nose to nose — a large tan
> retriever and a small cream terrier — on a sage green faceted grass disc.
> Matte clay and painted wood, warm cream background, soft studio light,
> shallow depth of field, wooden toy aesthetic. No text.

**Notes:** the two dogs *must* differ in size — the app matches on size and this
image is the product in one picture. Leave headroom above them; the site's
headline sits there. If the dogs come out facing the camera rather than each
other, add "seen from the side, in profile, facing each other."

## 4. Care hub still life

**Target: 3:2 landscape**

> A low-poly 3D render of a small still life arranged on a warm wooden shelf: a
> rounded ceramic pet food bowl in terracotta, a coiled sage green leash, a
> folded cream blanket, and a small potted succulent in a terracotta pot. All
> objects are built from visible flat polygon facets with a matte clay and
> painted wood finish — no gloss, no metal, no reflections. Warm cream
> background, softly blurred. Soft diffused studio lighting from the upper left,
> gentle contact shadows beneath each object. Shallow depth of field.
> Handcrafted wooden toy aesthetic, calm, warm and domestic. Wide landscape
> composition with the objects arranged along the shelf and empty cream space
> above. No text, no lettering, no labels, no words, no logos.

**Short (Meta AI):**
> Low-poly faceted 3D still life on a wooden shelf: terracotta pet food bowl,
> coiled sage leash, folded cream blanket, small potted succulent. Matte clay
> and wood, warm cream background, soft studio light, shallow depth of field.
> No text.

**Notes:** deliberately **no pills, no syringes, no medical instruments, no
first-aid cross**. The app describes published guidance and never prescribes —
a medical still life would visually promise something the product refuses to do.
If the model adds a medical item, regenerate. Domestic objects only.

## 6. Safety

**Target: 1:1 square · the hardest one to get right**

> A low-poly 3D render of a friendly dog sitting calmly behind a low wooden
> garden fence, seen from the front. The dog is built from visible flat polygon
> facets in warm cream and tan, with a matte clay finish, and looks relaxed and
> content. The fence is simple faceted painted wood in soft off-white, low
> enough to see the dog clearly over it, with a small sage green shrub and a
> terracotta plant pot beside it. Warm cream background, softly blurred. Soft
> diffused studio lighting from the upper left, gentle contact shadows. Shallow
> depth of field. Handcrafted wooden toy aesthetic, warm, calm and domestic —
> a safe garden, not a barrier. Square composition. No text, no lettering, no
> words, no logos.

**Short (Meta AI):**
> Low-poly faceted 3D render, a calm friendly dog sitting behind a low white
> wooden garden fence with a sage shrub and terracotta pot. Matte clay and
> painted wood, warm cream background, soft light, cosy domestic wooden toy
> aesthetic. No text.

**Notes:** the garden-fence idea is deliberately chosen over a shield or a lock.
A shield reads corporate-security; a lock or a camera reads surveillance, which
is the opposite of what this section says (the app *reduces* what strangers can
see about you). A relaxed dog in a safe garden is the right feeling. **The dog
must look relaxed, not trapped or sad** — if it reads as confined, regenerate
with "happy, relaxed, content, ears up."

## 7. Install

**Target: 16:9 wide · also the Open Graph image**

> A low-poly 3D render of a faceted smartphone standing upright on a warm wooden
> shelf, with a small cheerful dog in cream and tan sitting beside it looking up.
> The phone has a rounded faceted body in soft off-white and warm grey, and its
> screen is completely blank and empty in a flat pale colour with nothing on it —
> no interface, no icons, no text of any kind. Behind them, a warm cream wall
> with a second wooden shelf holding two small terracotta plant pots with faceted
> succulents, softly blurred. Matte clay and painted wood materials, visible flat
> polygon facets, no gloss, no reflections. Soft diffused studio lighting from
> the upper left, gentle contact shadows. Shallow depth of field. Handcrafted
> wooden toy aesthetic. Wide landscape composition with empty cream space to one
> side. No text, no lettering, no words, no user interface, no icons, no logos.

**Short (Meta AI):**
> Low-poly faceted 3D render, a smartphone with a completely blank empty screen
> standing on a wooden shelf, small cream dog sitting beside it, terracotta
> potted plants on a shelf behind. Matte clay and painted wood, warm cream
> background, soft light. Blank screen, no interface, no text.

**Notes:** **the blank screen is the whole point and it is the instruction these
models fight hardest.** Real screenshots get composited in afterwards, and a
generated fake interface would show a product that does not exist — which is
what made the supplied "PET SEARCH CHAT / AdoptBot" reference unusable. If the
screen comes back with any interface on it, regenerate. If it keeps happening,
add: "the phone is switched off and its screen is a plain flat empty rectangle."

Leave clear cream space on one side — this doubles as the 1200×630 OG image and
needs room for a headline.

## Delivering them — exact filenames

**Put every file in `marketing/public/art/`.** That directory does not exist
yet; create it. It sits beside `marketing/public/screens/`, which is where the
real app screenshots are copied to, and Vite serves everything under `public/`
from the site root — so a file at `marketing/public/art/hero.png` is served at
`/art/hero.png`, which is what the HTML references.

Name them **exactly** as below. The site has numbered `ART 1` … `ART 7`
placeholder slots in its HTML that correspond one-to-one with the pieces in
this document, so the numbers are already agreed — the filenames just have to
match.

| # | Filename | Ratio | Where it is used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `hero.png` | 4:3 | `index.html` — hero, the top of the site |
| 2 | `dog.png` | 1:1 | `index.html` — playdates section *(slot not placed yet)* |
| 3 | `park.png` | 1:1 | `index.html` or `/where-we-are` *(slot not placed yet)* |
| 4 | `care.png` | 3:2 | `care/index.html` — the hero of `/care` |
| 5 | `desert.png` | 4:3 | `where-we-are/index.html` — the hero of `/where-we-are` |
| 6 | `safety.png` | 1:1 | `safety/index.html` — the hero of `/safety` |
| 7 | `install.png` | 16:9 | `index.html` — the install/CTA section near the bottom |

**Two names differ from what you might expect:**

- **`desert.png`, not `arizona.png`.** The site's page is `/where-we-are`
  rather than `/arizona`, because `LAUNCH_REGIONS` is a list and a second state
  would make every "Arizona" in the filename and the route a small lie. The
  image is a desert; name it for what it is.
- **Pieces 2 and 3 have no slot in the HTML yet.** Generate and deliver them
  anyway under these names — the sections that use them are still being built,
  and having the files waiting is better than blocking on them.

### The Open Graph image

All four pages already reference `https://petpalsconnectapp.com/og.png`, and it
does not exist yet. This is the picture that appears when somebody shares a link
in a message, on Slack, or on social.

- **`marketing/public/og.png`** — note this one goes at the **root of
  `public/`**, not inside `art/`, because the pages reference `/og.png`.
- **Exactly 1200 × 630 pixels.** Not "about" — the platforms crop hard.
- Use **piece 7 (the phone and dog on a shelf)** cropped to 1200 × 630, or
  generate it again at that ratio. It is the one composed with empty cream
  space to one side, which is what a share card needs.
- **No text baked into it.** The platforms overlay the page title themselves,
  and any lettering in the image will collide with it.

### Format

- **PNG**, 2048px on the long edge for everything in `art/`.
- **Transparent background** for pieces 2, 4 and 6 if your tool can manage it;
  otherwise the cream ground is fine and the CSS handles it.
- Do not pre-compress or resize beyond that — the build generates the
  responsive sizes and modern formats from the original.

### What happens when you drop them in

The placeholder slots are deliberately ugly — a dashed box with the piece's name
in it — so a missing illustration looks missing rather than looking like a
design choice. When a file lands at the right path, the slot is replaced by the
image. If you deliver a file and the dashed box is still there, the filename is
wrong; check it against the table above character for character.

## Checking a generation before you keep it

1. **Are the facets visible?** Smooth = wrong. It should look carved.
2. **Is it matte?** Any shine, chrome or glass = wrong.
3. **Is the background warm cream**, not white and not grey?
4. **Is there any text anywhere?** Including on tags, signs and screens. Reject.
5. **Does it sit beside the Buddy dog reference** without looking like a
   different artist? That piece is the anchor.
6. **Piece 7 only:** is the screen genuinely blank?

## Marks

Six small pieces for the brand rather than the pages: candidates for the nav
mark, the favicon at larger sizes, the app-store listing icon and the OG
fallback card. Same style block as everything above. Deliver as square PNG,
1024px, into **`marketing/art/marks/<name>.png`** - the subfolder is
deliberate, `npm run art` only encodes the top level of `art/`, and nothing on
the pages references these yet, so they must not join the manifest until
something does.

Piece 5 is the exception: it **already exists as vector**, in
`marketing/public/favicon.svg` and as `MARK` in `marketing/src/partials.ts`
(`#c4703f` / `#d98d5f` / `#9d4f27` on `#f5efe3`). A raster of a flat two-tone
hexagon at 30px is strictly worse than the SVG the nav already draws, so do not
generate it unless the point is to check the generator's idea of the mark
against ours.

**Checking a mark** is the list above plus one: does it still read at 64px?

most likely to pass.

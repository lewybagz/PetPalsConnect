## Poisons

**Volume.** The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center handled more than 451,000
calls about toxic substances, plants and poisons in 2024 — a ~4% rise on the
year before. The 24-hour hotline is **(888) 426-4435**.
— ASPCA, *The Official Top 10 Toxins of 2024*,
https://www.aspca.org/news/official-top-10-toxins-2024

**Chocolate** made up 13.6% of exposures in 2024. Theobromine thresholds in
dogs: mild signs (vomiting, diarrhoea, excessive thirst) around **20 mg/kg**;
cardiotoxic effects **40–50 mg/kg**; seizures at **≥60 mg/kg**. Reported oral
LD50 for theobromine and caffeine is 100–200 mg/kg, but severe signs and death
occur well below that. Theobromine content varies enormously by product:
cocoa powder ~737 mg/oz, baking chocolate ~376 mg/oz, dark chocolate ~130–138
mg/oz, milk chocolate far less. Dogs clear theobromine over roughly 17.5
hours against 2–3 hours in humans, which is why it accumulates.
— Merck Veterinary Manual, *Chocolate Toxicosis in Animals*,
https://www.merckvetmanual.com/toxicology/food-hazards/chocolate-toxicosis-in-animals ;
ASPCA, as above.

**Xylitol** (in sugar-free gum, some peanut butters, baked goods, and some
medicines) is a leading component of the food-and-drink exposure category,
which ranked second overall in 2024.
— ASPCA, as above.

**Grapes and raisins** can cause gastrointestinal upset, lethargy, excessive
thirst, tremors, abdominal pain and, in severe cases, irreversible kidney
damage. No safe dose is established.
— ASPCA, as above.

**Lilies and cats: the highest-stakes plant in the house.** All species of
*Lilium* (true lilies) and *Hemerocallis* (daylilies) are lethally toxic to
cats. Every part is dangerous — flower, leaf, stem, pollen — and documented
cases include kidney injury from pollen alone, from licking pollen off the
coat, and from drinking vase water. GI signs begin 2–12 hours after ingestion;
kidney effects 12–96 hours. Treatment is early aggressive IV fluids and
typically 48–72 hours of hospitalisation.
— UC Davis Veterinary Medicine,
https://healthtopics.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/health-topics/feline/lily-toxicity-cats ;
FDA, *Lovely Lilies and Curious Cats*,
https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/lovely-lilies-and-curious-cats-dangerous-combination ;
International Cat Care, https://icatcare.org/articles/lily-poisoning-in-cats

> **Editorial note.** "Do not have lilies in a house with a cat" is one of the
> few pieces of pet advice that is genuinely absolute. Say it that way.

## Identification

**Microchips roughly double or vastly exceed unchipped return rates, and only
when the registration is current.** Lord et al. examined 7,704 microchipped
animals entering 53 US shelters between August 2007 and March 2008:

| | Not microchipped | Microchipped |
|---|---|---|
| Dogs returned to owner | 21.9% | **52.2%** |
| Cats returned to owner | 1.8% | **38.5%** |

Where a microchipped animal was *not* returned, the most common reason was
incorrect or missing owner information in the registry.

— Lord, Ingwersen, Gray & Wintz (2009), *Characterization of animals with
microchips entering animal shelters*, JAVMA 235(2): 160–167,
https://avmajournals.avma.org/view/journals/javma/235/2/javma.235.2.160.xml

**A chip is not a GPS tracker** and does nothing on its own — it is a number
that a scanner reads and a database resolves. The AAHA Universal Pet Microchip
Lookup Tool searches participating registries.
— AAHA, https://www.aaha.org/faqs/

## Body condition

**2022 APOP survey:** **59% of dogs and 61% of cats** were classified by
veterinary professionals as overweight or having obesity.

**The perception gap:** only **39% of dog owners and 45% of cat owners**
considered their own pet overweight. Only 46% of owners said their vet
discusses ideal weight annually; 12% said it never comes up.

— Association for Pet Obesity Prevention, 2022 survey,
https://www.petobesityprevention.org/2022 ; 2022 State of Pet Obesity Report,
https://www.petobesityprevention.org/2022-state-of-pet-obesity-report-summary

**Body condition scoring is hands-on, not visual**, and long or thick coats
and a cat's primordial pouch both make eyeballing unreliable. The standard
scales are 9-point (WSAVA/AAHA); 4–5 of 9 is ideal.
— APOP, as above.

## Choosing a professional

Dog training is **unregulated** in the US: anyone may use the title "trainer"
or "behaviourist". Independent certifications — issued by bodies that do not
also sell the training courses — are the meaningful signal.

- **CPDT-KA** (Certification Council for Professional Dog Trainers): ≥300
  hours of training experience in the past 3 years, an exam, and adherence to
  a code of ethics and a LIMA (least intrusive, minimally aversive) policy.
- **CPDT-KSA**: adds assessed video of real training sessions.
- **CBCC-KA** (CCPDT): behaviour consulting; ≥300 hours of behaviour
  consulting in 3 years plus a 200-question exam.
- **CDBC / CCBC** (International Association of Animal Behavior Consultants):
  advanced behaviour consulting for aggression, severe anxiety, phobias.
- **KPA CTP** (Karen Pryor Academy): a five-month program with written and
  practical assessment.
- **DACVB** (Diplomate, American College of Veterinary Behaviorists): a
  licensed veterinarian who completed a behaviour residency and board exam.
  The only one of these who can diagnose medical causes and prescribe.

— CCPDT/IAABC/KPA/ACVB summaries via
https://cattledogpublishing.com/blog/animal-trainers-and-behaviorists-licensing-and-certification/ and
https://baypathhumane.org/what-does-that-dog-training-certification-mean/

**Screening question that works:** ask what happens when the dog gets it
wrong. Per AVSAB, the answer should involve changing the setup or the
reinforcement, not applying something the dog wants to avoid.
— AVSAB Humane Dog Training Position Statement,
https://avsab.org/resources/position-statements/

## Adoption and settling in

**The "3-3-3 rule" (3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to learn the routine, 3
months to feel at home) has no research behind it.** **[weak]** It originates
in rescue practice, not study.

**What the data suggests instead:** in a 2025 adopter study only **30%** felt
their dog had settled within three months, and **41%** said adjustment took
four to six months. Reported estimates for full decompression commonly run
from six weeks to three months and beyond, and some behaviour can *worsen*
around month three as a dog relaxes enough to express itself.

— Discussion of the evidence and the 2025 adopter findings:
https://rescuedbytraining.com/2024/09/23/debunking-the-3-3-3-rule/ ;
https://www.thefarmersdog.com/digest/decompression-help-your-new-shelter-dog-feel-at-home/

> **Editorial note.** The rule's *shape* — expect a slow, non-linear settling,
> do not judge the dog by week one — is sound and humane. Its precision is
> invented. Write it as "the timeline is made up, the patience is not."

## Access: pet-friendly places

**Service animals under the ADA are dogs (and in limited cases miniature
horses) individually trained to perform tasks for a person with a
disability.** They must be allowed anywhere customers are permitted, including
dining rooms, bars, waiting areas and outdoor patios. No extra fee, no
segregation to a particular section.
— ADA.gov, *Service Animals*, https://www.ada.gov/topics/service-animals/

**Emotional support animals and therapy animals are not service animals under
the ADA** and carry no public-access right to restaurants and shops.
— ADA.gov, as above.

**A pet dog on a restaurant patio is at the business's discretion**, within
state and local health codes — some states permit dogs in outdoor seating
while barring them indoors, others delegate to local health departments.
Health codes never override ADA rights for service animals.
— WebstaurantStore summary of state rules,
https://www.webstaurantstore.com/blog/3812/service-animals.html ;
ADA.gov, as above.

> **Editorial note.** This matters to PetPalsConnect specifically: the seeded
> Location catalogue includes pet-friendly restaurants and cabins. "Listed as
> pet-friendly" is not "your dog may go inside", and the article should say
> to call ahead.

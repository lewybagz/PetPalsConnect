# Verified claims: rabbits, guinea pigs, birds, reptiles

These species make up a small share of PetPalsConnect's feature surface — the
app has no rabbit-shaped matching — but they are a large share of the care
questions their owners cannot get answered anywhere good. The commercial pet
trade is the loudest voice in all four categories and it is wrong about most
of the basics.

---

## Rabbits

**Diet is overwhelmingly hay.** RWAF guidance is roughly **85% hay, 10% leafy
greens, 5% pellets** — commonly summarised as "around 80% forage". Pellets are
a supplement measured in tablespoons, not a meal.
— Rabbit Welfare Association & Fund,
https://rabbitwelfare.co.uk/how-to-feed-rabbits/ and
https://rabbitwelfare.co.uk/hay-and-health/

**Hay is dental and digestive at once.** Continuous fibrous chewing wears
continuously growing teeth, and keeps the gut moving. A low-fibre diet leads
to both dental disease and gut stasis.
— RWAF, as above.

**GI stasis is an emergency, not a bad day.** If the gut stops moving the
system shuts down, and it is frequently fatal. A rabbit that has not eaten or
passed droppings for 12 hours needs a vet, not a wait-and-see.
— RWAF; *Good rabbit welfare means friends and forage*, Veterinary Practice,
https://www.veterinary-practice.com/article/good-rabbit-welfare-means-friends-and-forage

**Rabbits are social and most pet rabbits are kept alone.** RWAF's position is
that rabbits should live with a compatible rabbit companion wherever possible.
PDSA's PAW Report found 42% of rabbits live alone and a further 5% live only
with another species — an estimated 530,000 rabbits (48%) whose companionship
needs are not met.
— PDSA PAW Report 2023,
https://www.pdsa.org.uk/what-we-do/pdsa-animal-wellbeing-report/paw-report-2023/rabbits ;
RWAF, https://rabbitwelfare.co.uk/5-welfare-needs/

## Guinea pigs

**They cannot make their own vitamin C.** Requirement is at least **10 mg/kg
body weight per day**, rising to **30 mg/kg/day** for pregnant sows —
practically, roughly 20–25 mg/day for an adult and 30–40 mg/day for a growing
or pregnant animal. First signs of scurvy appear within **2–3 weeks** of
inadequate intake.
— Merck Veterinary Manual, *Nutritional Problems of Guinea Pigs*,
https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/nutritional-problems-of-guinea-pigs

**Signs of deficiency:** poor coat, poor wound healing and infection
resistance, reduced appetite, weight loss, lethargy, dental disease, bone
weakness, pain, bloody diarrhoea.
— Merck, as above.

**Vitamin C added to drinking water degrades** and cannot be relied on as the
sole source; it also makes water taste unpleasant, so animals drink less.
Fresh food and a stabilised supplement are the usual recommendation.
— Veterinary Partner, *Vitamin C Supplements for Guinea Pigs*,
https://veterinarypartner.vin.com/default.aspx?pid=19239&catId=102922&Id=4952750

**Space.** Commonly cited minimum for one guinea pig is about **7.5 sq ft
(0.7 m²)**, adding 2–4 sq ft per additional animal; welfare organisations
generally recommend considerably more, and boars need extra room to avoid
territorial conflict.
— Merck, *Providing a Home for a Guinea Pig*,
https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/guinea-pigs/providing-a-home-for-a-guinea-pig ;
Humane World for Animals,
https://www.humaneworld.org/en/resources/guinea-pig-housing-requirements

**Company.** Guinea pigs are highly social and should be kept in same-sex
pairs or groups.
— Burgess Pet Care,
https://www.burgesspetcare.com/pet-care/guinea-pigs/companionship-guinea-pig/

## Birds

**PTFE fumes kill birds, often before the owner notices anything.** Pyrolysis
of PTFE releases carbonyl fluoride, hydrogen fluoride and perfluoroisobutylene
— colourless and odourless. Pyrolysis is usually cited above **260°C (536°F)**,
but the lowest temperature at which a nonstick coating has been reported in
peer-reviewed literature to kill birds is **202°C (396°F)** — well within
normal cooking range. Acute death from respiratory failure is the most common
outcome; milder exposures show as rales, laboured breathing, ataxia,
depression or anxious behaviour.
— VCA, *Teflon (Polytetrafluoroethylene) Poisoning in Birds*,
https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/teflon-polytetrafluoroethylene-poisoning-in-birds ;
Environmental Working Group, *Canaries in the Kitchen*,
https://www.ewg.org/research/canaries-kitchen

**Sources beyond the frying pan:** self-cleaning ovens, PTFE-coated drip pans
under burners (which exceed 315°C/600°F within minutes of normal use), heat
lamp covers, irons, ironing board covers, some space heaters and hair
appliances.
— VCA, as above.

**Foraging is most of a wild parrot's day and almost none of a caged one's.**
Wild parrots are reported to spend roughly 4–8 hours a day foraging; a
conventional food bowl reduces that to under an hour.
— Grey parrot foraging enrichment research,
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168159123001582

**Feather-destructive behaviour affects an estimated 10% of captive parrots**,
and limited foraging opportunity is one hypothesised driver — the "redirected
foraging" hypothesis, which has been tested as a treatment approach in African
greys.
— Lumeij & Hommers, *Foraging 'enrichment' as treatment for pterotillomania*,
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168159107001669 ;
contrafreeloading study,
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10451555/

> **Editorial note.** Foraging enrichment reliably increases foraging time but
> has **not** been shown to reach wild time budgets or to eliminate
> feather-damaging behaviour. Do not sell it as a cure. Feather plucking needs
> an avian vet first — it has medical causes as often as behavioural ones.

## Reptiles and amphibians

**Salmonella is the central household risk.** The CDC advises that reptiles
and amphibians are not recommended for households with children under 5,
people with weakened immune systems, or adults over 65, and that they should
not be kept in childcare centres. Everyone should wash hands thoroughly with
soap and warm water immediately after handling a reptile or amphibian or
anything from its enclosure, with adults supervising young children's
handwashing.
— CDC, *Reptiles and Amphibians — Healthy Pets, Healthy People*,
https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/about/reptiles-and-amphibians.html

**The four-inch turtle rule.** Since 1975 the FDA has banned US sale of
turtles with shells under 4 inches, specifically because small turtles end up
in small children's mouths.
— FDA, *Pet Turtles: A Source of Germs*,
https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/pet-turtles-source-germs

**Do not clean enclosures in the kitchen sink** or anywhere food is prepared;
use a dedicated tub and disinfect afterwards.
— CDC, as above.

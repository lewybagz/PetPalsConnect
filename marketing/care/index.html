For the pets already asleep on your sofa.

The care hub does not need a single match to be useful, and it does not need you to be in Arizona. Every pet in the house gets one, whatever species they are.

What is in it

Care places near you

Vets, groomers, pet shops, boarding. A phone number and a route to each, which is what you actually want at the moment you are looking.

Picks for your pet

Food and supplies by species, life stage and size. Categories of thing with a search link, never a named product we are paid to mention.

Records and reminders

Vaccination dates, flea and worming cycles, vet visits, microchip and licence numbers. The repeating ones remind you before they lapse.

Weight over time

A number, a trend and a chart. It shows you the line and leaves the judgement to your vet, where it belongs.

The poison lookup

The ASPCA's poison centre took about 451,000 calls in 2024, and food and chocolate are near the top of the list every year. So there is a table you can search, and it works offline once you have opened it once.

It answers one question: is this dangerous. It tells you whether published guidance says ring now, ring for advice, or keep it away from them.

It will never tell you how much is too much, and it does not have a "should I worry" branch. That judgement is exactly what the helpline exists to make, so both numbers sit above the search box, before you have typed anything.

If what you searched is not in the table, it says so, and it still gives you the numbers. A near-miss on this screen is worse than no answer at all.

Where the line is

Everything in here describes published guidance and stops there. No doses, no thresholds, no symptom checker, nothing you could act on without a vet. Health articles say which guideline they are quoting and what year it is from, because guidance moves.

If you write something in the special-needs box on a pet's profile, the app stops recommending food for them and shows you your local vets instead. "Diabetic" is a conversation with a professional, not a filter on a shopping list.

And sixty articles

Written from sourced research rather than assembled from the internet. Every number has a name attached to it, and where the evidence is thin the article says that out loud. The puppy five-minute rule and the shelter three-three-three rule are both in there, both flagged, with what the evidence actually is sitting next to them.
